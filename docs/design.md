# Design and limits

The recommended runner is `lob_sim.engine.run_causal_execution`. It combines a
synthetic market-message stream, an independently timed feed-delivery stream, and
one parent order. Its purpose is to make the timing and accounting of execution
inspectable.

## Input contract

`MarketMessage` supplies a market-state time, unique event index, continuous integer
sequence, and either a full-depth snapshot or absolute-quantity level updates.
`FeedDelivery` refers to a message by event index and supplies its receive time.
Deliveries may be missing, repeated, or out of order, but may not precede their
message's market-state time. All times share one known nanosecond origin.

The declared market interval must cover the execution interval. A quiet interval
means the synthetic source declares no changes there; silence in incomplete real
data would not justify the same conclusion. One instrument and one session are
modeled. Price and quantity inputs use `Decimal`, not binary floating point.

`ParentOrder` specifies side, quantity, an advance reference price, and a nonnegative
fee rate in basis points. `CausalConfig` specifies the execution interval, fixed
order and report latencies, optional price limit and freshness thresholds, recovery
settings, and depth rule. `StrategySpec` selects immediate execution, cumulative
TWAP, or current-cost-gated TWAP. The latter is a rule using the current visible
estimate, not a forecast or an optimal execution policy.

## Separate state and information

| State | Changes when | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Raw market book | A market message becomes effective | Source truth and terminal midpoint |
| Actual execution ledger | Messages update executable depth or an order arrives | Shared depth consumption and actual fills |
| Local feed book | Received messages can be accepted or recovered | Market information available to the strategy |
| Known order ledger | An order is submitted or its terminal report is received | Reservations and fills known to the strategy |

A decision receives a frozen local view and the known order ledger. Hidden market
changes and unreceived execution reports do not enter that view. The full result
trace includes market and actual states for subsequent inspection; it is not an
input to the policy. Earlier decision records are not rewritten after recovery.

Sending a child reserves parent quantity. This reservation prevents duplicate
allocation, but locks neither a price nor market liquidity. At arrival, the IOC
consumes the then-executable opposing depth within its price limit. The unfilled
remainder does not become a resting order. BUY prices may equal the maximum limit;
SELL prices may equal the minimum limit.

The actual ledger records execution at arrival. The known ledger keeps the child's
full reservation until it receives the terminal report, even when actual execution
was partial or zero. Processing that report records known fills and releases the
entire child reservation; duplicate reports are idempotent. Fees apply once to
filled notional, with the same positive cost sign for BUY and SELL.

## Time and deterministic ordering

Events use a priority queue ordered by time, event priority, and insertion order.
For an equal timestamp, priorities are:

1. Market messages, ordered by event index.
2. Snapshot-service reads.
3. Feed and snapshot deliveries.
4. Child-order arrivals.
5. Terminal execution reports.
6. Strategy decisions.
7. Recovery timeouts.
8. Deadline.

Zero-latency events are created by their causal parent before being queued. They
cannot occur before that parent; once queued, their priority applies to remaining
events. This is a declared simulator contract, not an assertion about exchange
internals.

An order with `decision_time + order_latency > deadline` is not sent. Equality
allows execution at the deadline. After the deadline, the engine receives only
reports already generated by executed orders. It does not extend market replay or
make additional decisions. A report received after the deadline can still describe
a fill executed within it. Scheduled response time and actual receive time are
distinct fields; early termination can leave the latter absent.

Optional catch-up targets the remaining parent quantity at the last decision time
that can arrive by the deadline. It can bypass a soft cost gate, but not book
validity, quantity availability, price protection, or the deadline.

## Recovery and unknown execution

Market validity and local-feed validity are separate. An invalid local book pauses
new book-dependent decisions; it does not cancel orders already in flight. If an
order arrives when the market book is invalid, its execution is unknown and the
run stops. It retains prior known fills and unresolved reservations, records the
unknown order, and returns `metrics=None`. It does not invent a zero fill.

`LocalRecovery` retains a trusted book, a sequence high-water mark, and a bounded
buffer of updates. A candidate snapshot is published only when it joins a
continuous sequence through the high-water mark. Publication is atomic and cannot
move the published sequence backward. Late missing updates can also restore a
continuous chain from a trusted anchor. Content faults, timeouts, or overflow
require a new qualifying snapshot. Resetting the buffer does not erase the
high-water mark.

The optional synthetic snapshot service reads the valid raw market book after a
configured read latency and returns it after a separate response latency. Incremental
messages may continue to arrive in between. A service snapshot repairs local
observation; it does not replenish executable market liquidity. Requests and retries
have an explicit maximum.

Receive age and source-state age are measured separately. Duplicate updates do not
make old information fresh. The update-payload buffer is bounded, but identity
fingerprints and provenance can grow with the message stream. No bound is claimed
for total engine memory. This is feed recovery, not disk checkpointing or recovery
of missing private execution history.

## Externally prescribed depth

Own fills consume shared executable depth, but do not alter subsequent public
messages. Two rules specify how to combine those fills with the external stream:

| Rule | Treatment of previous own fills |
|---|---|
| `persistent_debit` (default) | Keep cumulative debits against subsequent raw quantities |
| `refresh_touched` | Reset a price-level debit when an external update touches that level; a full external snapshot resets all debits |

The strategy estimates available depth from its local raw book and reported fills.
It cannot read hidden market debits. Under `refresh_touched`, a later received
external refresh supersedes an earlier fill debit; a delayed report cannot revive
that superseded debit. Consequently, locally estimated depth need not equal actual
executable depth. Neither rule models participant feedback, and neither is a
claimed upper or lower bound on real execution outcomes.

## Cost, completion, and missing values

Let `Q` be target quantity, `q` filled quantity, `R = Q - q`, `P0` the advance
reference price, `N` filled notional, `F` fees, and `s` be `+1` for BUY or `-1` for
SELL. For a valid raw terminal midpoint `M`:

```text
covered_cost     = s * (N - q * P0) + F
opportunity_cost = s * R * (M - P0)
is_cash          = covered_cost + opportunity_cost
is_bps           = 10000 * is_cash / (Q * P0)
completion_ratio = q / Q
```

The midpoint is taken from the trusted raw market book at the deadline, independently
of local-feed validity and own depth debits. Later snapshots cannot fill in a missing
terminal state. Full completion makes opportunity cost zero without needing a
midpoint. With remaining quantity and no valid two-sided terminal book, opportunity
cost and total IS are unavailable and a reason is recorded. Zero target quantity
makes the ratios undefined. Unknown execution makes the entire metrics record
unavailable.

For the delayed-report example, `Q=12`, `q=4`, `N=404`, `P0=M=100`, and `F=0`:
covered cost is 4, opportunity cost is 0, and completion is one third. That is a
partial execution with defined IS, not evidence of superior trading performance.

Accounting uses explicitly bounded Decimal contexts, validates finite values, and
rejects unsupported precision ranges. Ratios use their defined rounding precision.
JSON serializes Decimal values as strings, timestamps and identifiers as integers,
and missing values as `null`.

## Module boundaries and tradeoffs

| Module group | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| `lob_lab.costs`, `book`, `valuation`, `execution` | Depth, validation, fees, and parent/child accounting |
| `lob_lab.policy` | Frozen policy views, current-cost gates, and quantity decisions |
| `lob_lab.replay`, `timed_execution` | Shared message types, validation, schedule, depth ledger, and metrics |
| `lob_sim.models`, `recovery`, `engine` | Separate clocks and states, recovery, deterministic orchestration |
| `lob_sim.demo` | Two built-in synthetic inputs and JSON serialization |

The support modules retain an older `run_timed_execution` runner and replay query
functions. They are importable, but this project's documented execution interface
is `run_causal_execution`. The older runner uses a visible-clock proxy and synchronous
confirmation semantics; its behavior must not be confused with the causal runner.
Keeping those modules intact avoids splitting shared accounting and scheduling
logic during extraction. It trades a smaller refactor for some retained support
code.

The tests target hand-calculated quantity and cost expectations, hidden-information
perturbations, immutable historical views, and timing/recovery boundaries. Package
checks use the installed wheel from outside the source directory. Synthetic tests
establish behavior within this input contract; they do not establish performance,
exchange fidelity, or suitability for live trading.
